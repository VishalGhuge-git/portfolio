## Context: how a show goes live

Partner publishers delivered their shows through **APIs that the publishers managed**. Content operations **received that content and made sure it went live on time and to standard**. If a show was late or wrong, the viewer saw it: a delay hurts the user experience and **hurts watch time**, and the catalogue drives 30-40% of it.

## The problem

The pipeline had **a high backlog, no process and no plan**. Content was piling up faster than it could go live, and the work ran on effort and firefighting.

## What I did

I led and managed the team and **set up the process flow** so that work moved in one defined path with clear timelines, instead of case by case.

- **Built and trained a team of 6** to run 300,000+ content units across publishers including Voot, ALTBalaji, Hungama Play and ShemarooMe
- **Defined the process flow** for receiving, checking and publishing content
- **Owned the timelines** so content went live on schedule

## Overhauling the API workflow

The API team's work had **no defined process, manual creation of data and old features**. I led an overhaul that fixed the workflow first, and brought in new capabilities through the **China tech team**, who built new CMS features alongside us.

- **+50% daily content output within one month**, measured by me
- **Workflows standardised across 7 teams**
- New **CMS capabilities** in place

## Onboarding ALTBalaji: 56 shows in a week

Normally **one show took about 3 days** to go live, because shows moved through onboarding one at a time. I brought the time down by **running the onboarding in parallel**, using **prioritisation and impact assessment** to decide what to start first and how to split the work. The result was all **56 shows from zero to live in one week**, and the approach was **adopted as the team standard** for onboarding publishers.
