**TL;DR** Episode 1 of a short animated series about a product manager who, every night by a campfire, tells his small wise bird Garu what went wrong at work. I designed the characters, the story and the production workflow with AI tools. The episode's lesson is a real product one: users asked for more filters, so the team built more filters, and nobody used them, because what users wanted was to trust the answer.

## Context and user problem

I wanted a creative, distinctive way to share what I learn as a product manager, in a calm, old-school style with its own character, rather than another talking-head video. The brief to myself: one episode first, before committing to a series.

## The characters

Two personas: **the builder and product manager** (me, with a laptop by the fire) and **Garu**, a small, cute bird inspired by Garuda from Indian mythology, who has "been around for three thousand years". Garu sounds wise, not hyper, and his voice matches his small, young look. The episode uses two voices.

## Decision and rationale

I treated the episode as a product with a hard quality bar: it should match or beat the 3D low-poly clay reels that inspired it. The video also shows how it got there, in four tries:

| Problem | What I did |
| --- | --- |
| **Garu would not stay Garu.** Every shot produced a slightly different bird. | Made a **character sheet** (front, side, back), like a real animation studio |
| **The AI added an Apple logo to the laptop** that nobody asked for. | Removed it, with a before and after shown in the video |
| **My first version was basically a slideshow.** | Rebuilt the shots so that everything moves |
| **My first draft made no sense.** | Rewrote the script three times |

## What I shipped

A finished 76-second Episode 1, with a story, two voices, subtitles and a behind-the-scenes section on how it was made.

## What is next

The episode ends by asking whether I can make one episode in one hour. That is the goal I am working towards with this toolset, not something I claim to have achieved yet.

## What I would do differently

I would decide upfront how to judge the first result, for example views, saves and follows, so that the decision to continue to a series rests on a signal and not a feeling.
