## Context

Foyr Neo is Foyr's existing interior design software. Ideate was a bet that AI could take over the slowest, most subjective part of the work: **exploring ideas** before any detailed design begins.

## Users and their jobs

| User | Job to be done |
| --- | --- |
| **Designers** (existing Foyr customers) | Explore and show design directions quickly, without building each one by hand |
| **Homeowners** | See what their own space could look like, in an old room or a completely new one |

## The problem

Ideating is where design work stalls. A designer has to build every option before a client can react to it, and a homeowner can't picture a result from a floor plan or a description. The goal was to make **designing and ideating easier**, whether the space is an existing room or a blank one.

## The decision that shaped the business: pricing

An AI feature has a real cost every time it runs, so pricing could not be an afterthought. I devised and planned the plans alongside the MVP, because the plan structure decides who can use the product and whether it can sustain itself.

The model is **freemium with a credit allowance**:

| | **Free** | **Pro** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Price** | $0 per month | $15 per month (launch offer, list price $25) |
| **Credits** | 5 free credits | 60 credits per month |
| **Downloads** | Watermarked | No watermark |
| **Usage rights** | Not for commercial use | Commercial use |
| **Editing** | Limited-period access to edit design | Full access: add, replace and remove products with AI, change wall paint and wallpaper, one-click enhance |

How the plans work as a funnel:

- **Credits cap the cost.** Every generation spends a credit, so the free tier can't run up unlimited AI cost.
- **Free is enough to feel the value.** A few credits let someone see their own room transformed before paying.
- **The upgrade triggers are the moments of real intent:** removing the watermark, using a design commercially (what professional designers need) and the full editing tools.
- **Monthly and yearly billing** are both offered.

**What drove the numbers.** I sized the Pro allowance and price on three inputs: the **AI cost per generation** (each credit has to cover its compute), **competitor pricing** (benchmarked against similar AI design tools), and **typical designer usage** (how many designs a working designer makes in a month).

**What I rejected.**

| Option | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| **Pay-per-use only** | Unpredictable spend puts people off experimenting, and it gives no recurring revenue to plan against |
| **No free tier** | People would pay before seeing their own room redesigned; a free taste is the fastest way to show the value of an AI product |

## Design language

The interface is deliberately quiet so the generated rooms carry the colour: neutral surfaces, pill-shaped controls, and a single gradient reserved for action and emphasis.

## Design language

The visual language of the product: quiet neutrals, one blue-to-orange gradient reserved for action and emphasis, pill-shaped controls and soft cards. The gradient does the branding, so the generated rooms stay the colour on the page.

## Working with an external agency

I directed an external UI/UX agency in Figma. The most useful lesson was where to spend attention: I gave **direction and explained the vision**, and focused on the **high-level UI direction, not small friction points**. Chasing details early would have slowed the team and hidden the big decisions.

## Outcome

- **MVP in 5 months**, ready for handoff, with the AI interaction patterns validated through prototype testing
- A working product live at [ideate.foyr.com](https://ideate.foyr.com), with a marketing site at [foyrideate.ai](https://www.foyrideate.ai)
